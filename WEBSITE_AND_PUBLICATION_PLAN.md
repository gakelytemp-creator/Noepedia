# Noepedia Website and Public-Discourse Plan

> **Status:** content and information-architecture specification.  
> No site code should be written until this structure is accepted.

---

## Purpose

The website should serve two audiences without mixing their standards:

### Academic visitor
Wants:
- definitions;
- mechanism;
- prior literature;
- testability;
- experimental plan;
- limitations;
- references.

### Public visitor
Wants:
- why this matters;
- what problem is being addressed;
- understandable metaphors;
- social and economic consequences;
- a clear route to deeper material.

The website should therefore have **two doors into one project**.

## Section 5 — Safety Audit

A dedicated page should list:

### Risks the architecture is designed to reduce
- opaque unsupported completion;
- silent model revision;
- untraceable provenance;
- concentration of expensive cognition;
- repeated reconstruction of settled knowledge;
- invisible loss of representational distinctions;
- hidden goal formation.

### Risks the architecture may introduce
- false closure;
- ontology lock-in;
- corrupted provenance;
- over-trust in explicit structure;
- Daimonion failure;
- centralized control of the shared field;
- surveillance / access-control abuse;
- social over-reliance on one epistemic infrastructure.

Each risk should have:
- mechanism;
- detection path;
- mitigation;
- residual uncertainty.

## Visual Language

The public cover can use a **diffraction / interference pattern** as the central image.

Meaning:

- multiple sources;
- interacting waves;
- structured pattern emerging from overlap;
- constructive and destructive interference;
- no single beam owns the result.

This is appropriate for the public title **Interference Pattern**.

The academic pages should stay visually restrained.

---

## Publication Sequence

1. freeze academic scope;
2. run safety / overclaim audit;
3. produce academic draft;
4. produce public essay from the academic draft;
5. build GitHub Pages site;
6. publish repository + site together;
7. prepare outreach packets;
8. contact researchers, podcasters, bloggers, and technical communities;
9. collect criticism;
10. convert criticism into OPEN / MISMATCH entries and revise.

---

## Non-Negotiable Publication Rule

The public version may be stronger in language, but it must never contradict the academic version.

> **Marketing may widen the doorway. It must not move the walls.**
